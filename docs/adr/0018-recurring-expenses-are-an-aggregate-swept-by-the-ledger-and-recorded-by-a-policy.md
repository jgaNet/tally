# 18. Recurring expenses are an aggregate, swept by the ledger, and each day is recorded by a policy

## Context

Request 23 of Tally (a recurring expense is set up by a member, and the background job records its expense on its day of every month,
once, whatever the servers, and never at a read; it ends when its payer or a member of its split has left, and is stopped by its payer or
the owner) was stormed before the code (`conventions/storms/recurring-expenses.yaml`, decided with the product's owner). The storm puts
the new thing in Ledger: its own aggregate, `RecurringExpense`, that records the days that have come, and a policy on each of those days
that records the expense the day stands for. Groups' link to Ledger (`GroupMembers`, ADR 6) already carries what the storm asks of
membership; no relationship is new.

The Lite profile (ADR 40) holds the aggregates and the domain events where a trigger fires, and says they come in by an ADR. Ledger's
`several-writers` and `reacts-to-domain-event` triggers already fire (the expenses are written by their payer and the owner, and
`ExpenseRecorded` is reacted to), so this decision is the ADR those rules ask for, and it adds no rule to the profile.

Nothing in the application runs on a timer except the debt sweep (ADR 14), and a read must never record an expense. A day that has come
is found only by a run that looks at it.

## Decision

- **A `RecurringExpense` is an aggregate of Ledger** (`src/Contexts/Ledger/Domain/RecurringExpense/RecurringExpense.ts`): its group, its
  payer (the member who set it up), its description, its amount in the group's currency, the split as it was given (`splitBetween`,
  `splitExactly` or `splitByShares`, kept as a `SplitDraft`, not decided), its `startsOn` and its `next`, the day of the next expense it
  will record. `setUp` validates what a recording validates (the description, the amount, the split as an expense's own rules decide it)
  and what is specific to it: `startsOn` is a real day of the calendar, no more than 365 days before today nor after, whose day of the
  month is 1 to 28. It generates its identity and records `RecurringExpenseSetUp`. `stop` records `RecurringExpenseStopped`. Both are
  pure: the time is given.
- **The background job is a use case of Ledger, `SweepRecurringExpenses`**, run by the composition root every `JOBS_INTERVAL_SECONDS` with
  the one clock (as the debt sweep of ADR 14 is). It loads every recurring expense whose `next` is no later than today (UTC) and, for each,
  asks `GroupMembers` who is in the group now. The aggregate's `sweep` decides from that answer:
  - if the group is not there, or its payer or a member of its split is no longer a member, it records `RecurringExpenseEnded`, and no
    expense is recorded for it; the use case removes it, so it is gone from every read;
  - otherwise it records one `RecurringExpenseDayCame` for each day from its `next` up to today, each with the id of the expense that
    day stands for, and moves its `next` past today. A run that finds a backlog (a `startsOn` already passed) records each day that has
    come, in one save.

  The sweep saves over the version it was read at (CONC-OPTIMISTIC): of two servers sweeping one database, the one that loses finds the
  days recorded and its command runs again on what was committed (`CONFLICT_ATTEMPTS`), so each day is recorded once, however many servers
  run and wherever one is stopped. Nothing is decided by a read: `GetRecurringExpense` and `GetGroupRecurringExpenses` change nothing.
- **Each day's expense is recorded by a policy on `RecurringExpenseDayCame`** (`RecordRecurringExpenseHandler`, in Ledger's `Application/
  Events`). It runs in its own transaction (EVENT-AFTER-COMMIT, ADR 12): it reads whether an expense with the day's id is already there, and
  if not, it records one with that id, dated the day, paid by the payer, with the description, amount and split of the recurring expense,
  through `Expense.register`, given the id (the same checks as a recording, and the split decided by the rule in force now). The expense's
  `ExpenseRecorded` then goes to Notifications and the balances like any other expense (request 17). The check on the id makes a redelivery
  record nothing twice.
- **Identity is born once** (DOMAIN-IDENTITY): the id of a day's expense is generated by the sweep's decision, carried by
  `RecurringExpenseDayCame`, and the policy builds the expense on that id. `Expense.register` takes that id as its last argument and generates one only when none is given, so a recording a member makes keeps its own
  id as before.
- **Membership is asked at the run.** The sweep judges a day by who is in the group when the run looks at it, not when the day came, since
  Groups keeps no date of leaving that Ledger could read: a payer or split member who left before the run ends the recurring expense,
  and its past days are not recorded. The window between the sweep's decision and the policy's recording is a few instants: if a member
  leaves inside it, the policy's recording is refused by the expense's own rule, nothing is recorded for that day, and the failure is logged
  by the reaction. A refusal is not retried, since the expense could not be recorded in any case.
- **The fragment and the routes**: `api/23-recurring-expenses.yaml` is the API; `POST /groups/{id}/recurring` (a member sets it up; the caller
  is its payer), `GET /recurring/{id}` and `GET /groups/{id}/recurring` (a member of the group reads it; to anyone else it does not exist),
  `DELETE /recurring/{id}` (its payer or the group's owner; another member `403`; to anyone else it does not exist). The reads answer the
  split as it was given, and `next`.
- **No migration.** The table `recurring_expenses` is created by `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS`, as every table of this application is.
- **`JOBS_INTERVAL_SECONDS`** runs both sweeps, the debt sweep of ADR 14 and this one.

## Consequences

- A day is recorded at the first run on or after it, not at the instant it comes: a run every `JOBS_INTERVAL_SECONDS` (60 seconds by
  default) bounds the delay.
- A recurring expense whose `startsOn` is in the past records every day that has come at the next run, and that run may record many
  expenses at once. Each run reads every recurring expense that is due, which is fine at this application's size, and the first thing to
  index (by `next`, which is indexed) if it grows.
- Stopping and ending both remove the aggregate's row: a recurring expense that is not there gives its payer or owner a `404`, and an
  expense it recorded stays, edited or deleted like any other.
- The reaction that records an expense shares the expense's rules: a day whose split no longer fits is refused in the reaction, and the
  recurring expense has already moved past it, as the window above says.

## When to revisit

When membership needs a date (a member who left on the 3rd of a month, and a recurring expense that should stop then and not at the next
run), which needs Groups to say when a member left; when the sweep's cost grows with the recurring expenses rather than with the days due;
and when a day that a refusal of the reaction lost should be retried or told to its payer instead of logged.
