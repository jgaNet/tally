# 19. Each context writes the audit entries of its own changes of money, and Ledger reads them through two ports (no new context, no new relationship)

## Context

Request 28 gives every group an audit: each change of money is an entry, with who made it, when, what, and how much, never
changed, and a member reads the group's audit, the oldest first. The changes are made in three contexts: Ledger records, edits
and deletes expenses (and a recurring expense's day), Settlements records cash settlements and the provider's word about a
payment and its refund, and Groups passes a balance on when a member leaves. The storm `audit`, decided with the product's
owner, chose that each context writes its own entries in the transaction of its change, from its aggregate's facts, and that
Ledger's audit query reads the other two through ports it owns over their read models, on the relationships the map already
has: `SettlementAudit` (Settlements → Ledger, the link that carries `SettledAmounts`) and `PassedBalances` (Groups → Ledger,
the link that carries `GroupMembers`). No integration event is published for an entry: an entry must be there as soon as the
change is answered, which a fact relayed after the commit would not promise (T18). This ADR records what the storm left to the
code. No context and no relationship is added.

## Decision

- **Each aggregate keeps the entries of the changes it made** (`newAuditEntries`): `Expense` for an expense recorded, edited or
  deleted (`expense-recorded`, `expense-edited`, `expense-deleted`), `SettlementBook` for a settlement the balances start to count
  (`settlement-paid`: in cash when it is recorded, paid by its payee; through the provider when the provider says it succeeded,
  paid by its payer) and one they stop counting (`settlement-refunded`, when the provider says it is refunded, by its payee), and
  `Group` for a balance passed on when a member left (`balance-passed`, by and about the member who left, when it is not 0). The
  entry's identity is generated by the domain, and its time is the one the application's clock gives the aggregate: the same
  clock that already decides the change.
- **The entries are written by the repository that saves the aggregate**, in the same transaction and only when the save succeeds:
  a refused command, a concurrency conflict or a webhook that changes nothing writes none. Each context has its own table
  (`audit_entries` in Ledger, `settlement_audit` and `group_audit`), and no request updates or deletes a row.
- **A pending payment through the provider stopping is not an entry.** The refund of a payment that was still pending was never
  counted by the balances, so the provider's `refunded` for it changes no money the balances count and writes nothing. A `refunding`
  or `paid` payment refunded is counted and is an entry.
- **Ledger reads the audit** (`GetGroupAudit`, `GET /groups/{id}/audit`): its own entries through `IAuditQueries`, the settlements'
  through `ISettlementAudit` and the passed balances through `IPassedBalances`, both Ledger's ports, answered by adapters over
  Settlements' and Groups' read models (`SettlementsSettlementAudit`, `GroupsPassedBalances`). The entries are merged and sorted
  by their time, oldest first; the sort is stable, so entries written at the same instant keep the order of the contexts. A
  non-member finds no group, as for every read of a group.
- **The `at` of an entry is the time of the change**, dated by the clock when the change is decided, not by a provider's `at`.

## Consequences

- The audit is complete on the transaction of each change: a server killed after an answer has its entry, and two servers that
  race on the same change either write one entry (the one whose save succeeds) or none.
- Entries before this feature are not backfilled: the audit starts with the changes made after it is deployed. The storm records
  "from now on" for that reason.
- Ledger's audit query reads three read models in sequence, not in one transaction. An entry is written with its change, so a
  reader never sees a change without its entry in the same database file; it may see the entries of a change that is still
  being made by another request, and no more.
- The merged read carries no ids or ordering between contexts beyond time. Ties in time are ordered by the contexts' read order.

## When to revisit

When an audit must be complete across databases (the contexts then need one outbox per change), or when a change of money needs
to be reversed, which would be a new entry of its own kind, not an edit of an old one.
