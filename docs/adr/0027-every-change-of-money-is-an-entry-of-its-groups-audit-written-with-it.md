# 27. Every change of money is an entry of its group's audit, written with the change

## Context

Request 28: Ann swears the dinner was 60 euros, not 90. Who changed it, and when? Every change of money in a group becomes an entry of the group's
audit: who made it (`by`), when (`at`), what (`action`, about `subjectId`) and how much (`amount`). An expense recorded, edited or deleted; a
settlement paid (in cash when recorded, through the provider when the provider says it succeeded) or refunded (when the provider says so); the
balance a member who left passed to the owner, when it is not 0. Nothing else is an entry. An entry is never changed or removed. A member of the
group reads the audit, the oldest first; to anyone else the group does not exist.

The storm (`conventions/storms/audit.yaml`) was decided with the product's owner before any code. Its three hot spots are decided and this record
keeps them: (1) no integration event carries the entries; each context writes its own in the transaction of the change, from its aggregate's
facts, and Ledger's audit query reads the other two through ports it owns (T18); (2) `by` is the member the change acts for: a provider's payment
its payer, a refund its payee, a recurring expense's day its payer; (3) an entry follows a fact, and a fact is recorded once, so a webhook said
twice, or a change that records nothing, writes nothing.

The relationships the storm names already exist in the context map (`conventions/domain.yaml`): Settlements → Ledger (SettledAmounts, the port
Ledger owns) and Groups → Ledger (GroupMembers). The storm adds two contracts to them, carried by the code: Ledger owns `SettlementAudit`,
answered by Settlements, and `PassedBalances`, answered by Groups. No context or relationship is added, so this is a feature, not a change to the
building blocks.

## Decision

- **Each context writes its own entries, in the transaction of the change.** The entries are facts of the aggregate that changed, written by the
  store that saves it, after the store's own write succeeded, in the same transaction:
  - Ledger: `Expense.register` (`expense-recorded`, by its payer), `Expense.edit` (`expense-edited`, by the editor, the amount after the edit) and
    `Expense.remove` (`expense-deleted`, by the deleter, the amount it had). `SqliteExpenseRepository.save` and `remove` write them. A recurring
    expense's day records through `Expense.register` too, so its entry is by its payer.
  - Settlements: `SettlementBook.recordCash` (`settlement-paid`, by the payee who recorded it), `settleProviderPayment` when the provider says
    `succeeded` (`settlement-paid`, by the payer), and `refundedByProvider` (`settlement-refunded`, by the payee). `SqliteSettlementBooks.save` writes
    them in its `settlement_audit` table.
  - Groups: `Group.leave` with a balance other than 0 (`balance-passed`, by and about the member who left, the balance as it left, positive or
    negative). `SqliteGroupRepository.save` writes them in its `passed_balances` table.
- **Refund of a settlement that is still pending is not an entry.** A provider's `refunded` may overtake its `succeeded` (request 13). The balances
  never counted such a settlement, so a refund of it leaves them where they were and the audit records no `settlement-refunded` for it. The
  refund's status still moves the settlement to `refunded`, as before. A refund of a paid settlement, or one `refunding`, is an entry, since the
  balances counted it.
- **Ledger reads the audit through ports it owns.** `GetGroupAudit` (`Application/Audit.ts`) checks the caller is a member through `GroupMembers`
  (`NotFound` for anyone else), reads its own table (`IAuditQueries`, `audit_entries`), and reads `SettlementAudit` and `PassedBalances` through its
  ports, each answered by an adapter over the other context's read model (`SettlementAuditOfSettlements`, `PassedBalancesOfGroups`, opened in
  `Bootstrap/Fastify/readModels.ts`). The three lists are merged by `at`, each read oldest first; on the same instant the expenses' entries come
  first.
- **Entries are never changed.** No request writes an entry again: the tables are appended to by their stores, and read. An expense deleted, or a
  member who left, keeps its entries.
- **Identity.** Each entry's id is generated by the aggregate that made the change (`Id.generate()`), so the id is born with the entry and the store
  keeps it.
- **Time.** `at` is the clock the use case already passes to the aggregate (the one system clock), in ISO form.
- **Each store is its own table in the application's file**, as every context's tables are, so the entry and the change commit together or not at
  all, and a server killed after the answer keeps both.

## Consequences

- A change refused by a rule, a conflict the transaction retries, or a webhook for a payment already decided writes nothing: the rule and the store
  write in the same transaction, and a failed Result rolls it back.
- A former member's entries stay in the group's audit, read by the members who remain.
- The audit reads each source separately, so a change committed between two of the reads may appear in one source and not another; a reader sees a
  consistent prefix of each source, not one snapshot of the three. The audit is an append-only record, so a later read only adds entries.
- The `at` of each entry is the time Tally recorded the change; the balance-passed entry carries the leave's time, and each settlement its webhook's
  or its recording's.
