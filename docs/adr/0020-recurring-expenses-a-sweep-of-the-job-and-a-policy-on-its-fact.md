# 20. Recurring expenses: an aggregate of Ledger, a sweep of the background job, and a policy on the day's fact

## Context

Request 23 sets up an expense its payer pays every month (`api/23-recurring-expenses.yaml`): from `startsOn` on, on its day of every
month, an expense of the group is recorded for it, at the first run of the background job on or after that day; once, whatever the
servers; and it ends when its payer or a member of its split has left. The storm (`conventions/storms/recurring-expenses.yaml`) was
decided before the code: the job notices that a day has come, never a read; the recurring expense is an aggregate of Ledger; a day
that came is a fact the aggregate records, with the optimistic check of CONC-OPTIMISTIC letting one of two servers win; and a policy on
that fact records the expense in its own transaction, the expense's id carried by the fact so that a redelivery finds it recorded.

Nothing new is needed between contexts: Ledger already asks Groups who the members of a group are, through `GroupMembers` (ADR 5), and
the job is the composition root's, which already runs Notifications' sweep (ADR 15).

## Decision

- **`RecurringExpense` is an aggregate of Ledger**, in `Domain/RecurringExpense`: its payer, description, amount, `startsOn`, the day of
  its next expense (`next`) and its split as given (the three ways, request 7). `setUp()` generates its id and records
  `RecurringExpenseSetUp`; `recordDay(day)` moves `next` to the same day of the following month and records `RecurringExpenseDayCame`,
  which carries the id of the expense that day becomes, generated by the aggregate; `stop()` and `end()` record their facts, and the
  repository removes it.
- **The decisions are pure.** `decideSetUpRecurringExpense` checks the description and amount as an expense's are, `startsOn` as request
  23 says it (a real day whose day of the month is 1 to 28, and no more than 365 days from today in UTC), and the split as an expense's
  (its members are members of the group, the payer among them, the amounts or shares allowed). `decideSweepRecurringExpense` answers
  `ended` when the payer or a member of the split is not a member, and otherwise the days it falls on up to today, from `next` on.
  `decideStopRecurringExpense` lets the payer and the group's owner stop it, and no one else.
- **The sweep is a use case of Ledger, run by the background job.** `SweepRecurringExpenses` reads every recurring expense, asks
  `GroupMembers` for its payer (the payer is the one asking, so the answer is the group's members or nothing), decides, and saves each
  changed one in one transaction on the file. The composition root runs it at each interval before Notifications' sweep, in a context of
  its own, with no caller. A read never runs it.
- **Once, whatever the servers.** The sweep's transaction takes the database's write lock (ADR 17), so two servers on one file do not
  sweep at once; the save still checks the version it was read at (CONC-OPTIMISTIC), so a writer that did not is refused and the next run
  finds nothing due.
- **The day's expense is recorded by a policy.** `RecurringExpenseDayCame` is published after the commit, through the outbox, and
  `RecurringExpenseDayCameHandler` records the expense with the id the fact carries (`Expense.register` takes the id a recurring day was
  born with, and generates one otherwise). A redelivered fact finds that expense recorded and records nothing; the expense is split by the
  rule in force when it is recorded and is counted and notified like any other, through `ExpenseRecorded`.
- **Ending is a fact of its own.** `RecurringExpenseEnded` is recorded when a member has left; the recurring expense is removed, and no
  expense is recorded for the day that came.

## Consequences

- The outbox is still the process's in-memory one (ADR 14, ADR 24 of the reference). A fact the process stops before relaying is lost with
  it. For this feature that means: if a server stops after the sweep commits a day and before the policy records its expense, that
  expense is not recorded. It is never recorded twice, and the other days are unaffected. Closing this needs a durable outbox written in
  the same transaction as the state (the gap ADR 14 names), which is an architecture change of its own and is not made here.
- `Expense.register` gains an optional id, used only by the policy. The expense's own identity is unchanged everywhere else.
- A day that comes while its payer is gone is not recorded; the run ends the recurring expense, as request 23 says.
- `next` is read as the sweep left it: a read between two runs shows the day the last run set, which is at most one job interval behind.

## When to revisit

When the outbox is durable in the expense's own transaction (above), and when a recurring expense can be edited (the request does not ask
for it; an edit would be a second aggregate change to decide the same way).
