# 22. A recurring expense is an aggregate of Ledger, and the background job records each of its days once

## Context

Request 23: the rent is the same every month, and nobody should record it again. A member sets up an expense they pay every month
(a description, an amount, the split as an expense's, and the first day, `startsOn`, a real day of the calendar no more than 365 days
from today, whose day of the month is 1 to 28). On each of its days, from the month of `startsOn`, an expense of the group is recorded
for it, dated that day, paid by its payer, at the first run of the background job on or after the day. Each day has one expense, however
many servers run and however often one is stopped and started. A recurring expense whose payer or a member of its split has left ends,
recording no expense from then on, and its payer or the group's owner may stop it at any time.

The storm (`conventions/storms/recurring-expenses.yaml`) decided before any code: the recurring expense is an aggregate of its own in
Ledger; the background job, not a read, notices that a day has come; a day is recorded once through an optimistic check on the recurring
expense, and a policy on the fact that a day came records the expense in its own transaction; and a member who has left ends it. The
storm adds no context and no relationship between contexts: Ledger already asks Groups for its members (ADR 0004, ADR 0006) and
publishes `ExpenseRecorded` (ADR 0017).

## Decision

- **A `RecurringExpense` is an aggregate of Ledger** (`Domain/RecurringExpense`). It is set up and stopped by two commands, and read by
  two queries. Ledger's profile admits an aggregate where two commands write one thing (`several-writers`): the sweep and a stop both
  write it, so it is one. Its rules are its own: `setUp()` validates the description, the amount, the split (as an expense's, the payer
  among the members) and the first day; `stop()` is for its payer or the group's owner; `runDay()` is one step on its day.
- **The background job notices a day, never a read.** The composition root runs `sweepRecurringExpenseDays` at every
  `JOBS_INTERVAL_SECONDS`, beside the outbox relay and Notifications' sweep. It reads the recurring expenses whose next day has come
  (`dueOn`) and steps each one through `RecordRecurringDay`, one transaction per step, until its next day is still to come or it has
  ended. A `startsOn` already passed is caught up in one run, one fact per day. Nothing is recorded by a read.
- **A day is recorded once, through an optimistic check.** `runDay()` moves the next day on by a month and records
  `RecurringExpenseDayCame`, which carries the id of the expense that day records, generated by the aggregate (identity is the domain's,
  DOMAIN-IDENTITY). The store saves the moved recurring expense at the version it was read at (CONC-OPTIMISTIC), so two servers that
  decide the same day cannot both win: the loser re-reads, finds the day past, and records nothing. The fact leaves the transaction only
  after the commit (EVENT-AFTER-COMMIT, through the outbox).
- **The policy records the expense once, with the id the fact carries.** `RecurringExpenseDayCameHandler` looks for the expense first and
  records it only if it is not there, so a fact the bus hands again records nothing. `Expense.register()` accepts that id, given by a
  recurring expense's day and by no other creation; every other expense still generates its own. The split is the one given, computed by
  the rule in force when the day is recorded (request 10). Its members are the split's own, which the step checked to be current in the
  same transaction.
- **A member who has left ends it.** `runDay()` is given the group's current members, read through a port Ledger owns, `IGroupMembers.currentMemberIds`,
  which Groups answers through a read of its own (`currentMemberIdsOf`). The sweep has no caller, so the existing `membersOf`, which
  answers only for a member who asks, could not serve it. If the payer or a member of the split is not current, the step records
  `RecurringExpenseEnded`, deletes the recurring expense and records no expense.
- **Membership is read on the run that records the day, not on the day.** Groups keeps when a member left, but no history of its
  membership on a given day is read here. A catch-up run records every day already passed with the members current at that run, so a
  member who left between a missed day and the run ends the recurring expense as of that run. This is the cost of having no membership history
  in the read model; the storm's "when its day comes" is read as "when its day is recorded".
- **The API is request 23's.** `POST /groups/{id}/recurring` answers `201 { id }`, `GET /recurring/{id}` and `GET /groups/{id}/recurring`
  answer the recurring expense with the split under the name it was given, and `DELETE /recurring/{id}` answers `204`. A refused
  first day is `422 InvalidStartsOn`, a non-member is `404`, and a stranger to the payer and owner is `403`.

## Consequences

- No new context and no new relationship between contexts. The context map is unchanged: Ledger still reads Groups' members through its
  own port. The published language is unchanged: an expense the job records is announced by the `ExpenseRecorded` fact it already publishes.
- A recurring expense's expenses are ordinary expenses from the moment they are recorded: edited, deleted and counted like any other.
- The job's cost is one read of the due recurring expenses each run, and one transaction per due day. A server that is down records its
  days when it comes back, at its first run.
- Ledger's `GroupMembers` port gains one method, and Groups' read model one query, both caller-free. Each is a read of committed
  membership, like `everMemberOf`.

## When to revisit

When a member's membership on a past day must decide a recurring expense that was missed (then Groups must keep the days a member left,
and the catch-up reads them); when a recurring expense's amount or split must change (an edit is a new decision, and the expenses it recorded
stay as they were); and when the job must run for another reason than a timer (a recurring expense that must be recorded at the moment of its
day, not at the next run, would need a scheduler of its own).
